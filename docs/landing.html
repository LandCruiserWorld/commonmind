Built on CockroachDB

Why agent memory needs a database that never goes down.

An agent whose memory goes offline doesn't degrade gracefully — it stops. So Cortex stores its memory in the database designed to survive: resilient, globally consistent, and SQL-familiar.

🛡

Never loses your memory

Data is automatically replicated across multiple nodes for high availability and failover. Hardware failure or maintenance won't take your agents' memory away.

🌍

Resilient to the kill-the-node test

Survives disk, machine, rack, and even datacenter failures with minimal latency disruption and no manual intervention — which is why principle #10 is committed to on camera.

↔️

Scales horizontally

As your workforce of agents grows, add nodes. Captures, recalls, and the memory graph scale without a drop in performance.

✓

Strongly consistent, always

ACID transactions over a transactional and strongly-consistent store — every copy across nodes reflects the same state, so agents and humans never see contradictory memory.

📍

Multi-region & geo-partitioning

Reads near your agents for low latency, meets data-sovereignty requirements, and survives regional outages — an extra layer of data safety.

🧩

SQL you already know

PostgreSQL wire protocol and most Postgres syntax. Your engineers and your tools migrate onto Cortex without learning a new language.

The philosophy

Milestones, not noise.

We record everything — but we only alert you on the few moments where being told changes your outcome. Two regimes, one system.

Silent
Agent ran a safe step in the background — build, retry, query, a happy-path sub-task.
Recorded · never a ping
Alert
Crossed a milestone — a phase completed, or "needs your decision".
A push you can act on
Priority
Finished differently than expected — failure, delay, or an answer you'd want tonight.
Wake you if needed
It's a conversation, not a notice

Your phone is a control surface for your agents. Not a pager.

The push is the doorbell. The thread is the room. You don't just get told — you ask back, redirect, and decide. The whole exchange becomes memory the agent recalls next time.

Use case · live

A marketing agency that never forgets a client

Every account person and their AI captures client context — preferences, brand voice, campaign history, reporting cadence — into a shared per-client brain. Onboarding a new account lead takes days, not months, and brand voice never drifts when someone leaves.

  • Client memory — "what tone does this client hate?" answered instantly.
  • Onboarding — new team member asks the brain, not the person who knows.
  • No drift — brand guide and voice persist even as people leave.
  • Shared — one brain per client, private per-contributor on request.
Try it
$ cortex save "client prefers short, no-jargon copy"
$ cortex ask "what did Acme reject last quarter?"
$ cortex note "--client acme" "avoid 22:00 sends"
Use case · live on a Raspberry Pi

A Solana trading platform

An agent watches the market on a Raspberry Pi reachable over Tailscale, reads past picks and rationale from memory, and flags a high-stakes move to you on your phone before acting. Every trade decision is recorded for post-trade audit — so you always know why it acted.

  • Recall — "why did we buy this in March?" answered in ms.
  • Approve — a risky entry lands on your phone as an approval, not a surprise.
  • Audit — full trade rationale is transactional, replayable, provable.
  • Never down — the memory survives a region outage; the bot keeps its context.
Try it
$ cortex save "bought DOGE on momentum — avg 0.22, stop -2%"
$ cortex ask "why did we buy DOGE?"
$ cortex ask --approval "increase position? (risk +0.5%)"
Use case · live

Recording coding sessions, team-wide

Every engineer and their AI capture dev sessions automatically. A shared memory the whole team reads and writes. New hires ask the system — not the person who knows — and the company brain grows with every session.

  • Automatic — CLI hooks capture sessions with zero effort.
  • Shared — the whole team reads/writes one brain by namespace.
  • Onboarding — new hires get years of experience in their first week.
  • Brain health — see which teams are actively feeding memory.
